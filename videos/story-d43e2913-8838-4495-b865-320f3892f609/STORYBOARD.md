# Scene 2
Voiceover: "To understand why rate cuts are such a big deal, we gotta do a quick rewind. Remember 2022 and 2023? Inflation was running completely wild. To stop the economy from overheating, the Fed did what it always does: it cranked interest rates up to twenty-year highs. Higher rates mean higher borrowing costs. When borrowing gets expensive, people spend less, businesses hire less, and inflation—in theory—cools off. It’s basically like throwing a giant bucket of ice water on a hyperactive golden retriever."

Visual: "A timeline graphic sliding back to 2022. Cartoon grocery items with absurdly huge price tags float by. Then, a giant cartoon Fed lever gets pulled down, causing a giant bucket of ice water to splash onto a running dog who instantly freezes and looks confused."

# Scene 3
Voiceover: "And guess what? It actually worked. Inflation dropped from its terrifying peak of over 9% back down toward the Fed’s cozy 2% target zone. Meanwhile, the job market cooled off without completely collapsing. Central bankers call this the legendary 'Soft Landing'—stopping inflation without crashing the entire car through the windshield into a deep recession."

Visual: "A sleek cartoon airplane labeled 'US Economy' smoothly landing on a runway lined with fluffy pink pillows. Jerome Powell is in the cockpit wearing aviator goggles giving a thumbs up."

# Scene 4
Voiceover: "So, what actually happens when the Fed flips the switch and lowers rates? First off, borrowing gets cheaper across the board. That means mortgages, car loans, and credit card interest rates gradually start becoming less painful. Second, those high-yield savings accounts that were paying 5% interest? Yeah, those yields will start dropping too. And third, money gets restless. When holding cash yields less, investors tend to move money back into stocks, real estate, and broader business investments."

Visual: "A split-screen animation: On the left, a credit card bill physically shrinks in size with a cheerful sound effect. On the right, a cartoon piggy bank looks sad as a 5% tag turns into a 3% tag, while an arrow points up toward a rising stock chart."
